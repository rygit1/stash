Stash — Q&A Battle Card

INC Pitch · Sat 2026-06-13 · Gavin · Ryan · Luca

Under fire: answer in one breath, then stop. Short + confident beats long + defensive.
• Stay Robin Hood — say "featured offers," "how it spreads," never "targeted ads / sell data / CAC."
• Never "steal/illegal" — say companies are "banking on you forgetting."
• Don't know it? "Great question — here's how we'd find out." Never bluff.

Tier 1 — Most likely + most dangerous

Why would anyone give you access to their email? That's scary.
"Totally fair — that's the first thing people ask. Stash is read-only. We can't send, delete, or change anything — we only look for gift cards. You sign in through Google, so we never see your password, and you can disconnect in one tap. Same Google sign-in you already use for a hundred apps."
How do I know you're not reading my personal emails / selling my data?
"We never sell your data — the whole brand is built on that. We only request the read-only gift-card scope, and Google enforces it — we couldn't read your personal mail if we wanted to. Our money comes from featured offers and our shop, not your inbox."
What happens to the data after you scan?
"Your cards live on your device, not our server. We're not building a profile. Encrypted in transit, stored on your phone, revocable anytime."

Never claim "end-to-end encrypted" (false). Say "encrypted in transit + stored on your device."

What's stopping Google / Apple / Amazon from just building this?
"They could build the feature — but it'd be a footnote in a giant app. For us it's the whole company, and we start where they won't bother: students, one school at a time. By the time it's worth their attention, we own the network. Same way Venmo beat the banks."
How are you different from Gyft / Prezzee / CardCash?
"Every one of them starts with a card you already know you have. Gyft makes you upload by hand. Prezzee just sells you new ones. CardCash buys yours for less than they're worth. Stash is the only one that finds the money you forgot about — automatically."
Isn't this easy to copy?
"The feature, maybe. The network, no. Once your whole friend group is on Stash, a copycat starts at zero. And the more inboxes we scan, the smarter the finder gets. First-mover plus a product that improves with every user."
Isn't it just a reminder app?
"You can't remind yourself about money you forgot you had. We find it first — the part you can't do yourself — then delete every excuse not to use it. Cal AI didn't invent calorie counting; it deleted the friction."
Doesn't Apple Wallet already do this?
"Apple Wallet only holds what you manually add. It has no idea about the $50 Nike card buried in your inbox from two birthdays ago. We find what you'd never think to add."

Tier 2 — Business model & money

How do you actually make money?
"Two ways. Today — featured offers from brands you'd actually use. Next — the Stash Shop, where you buy and send gift cards in-app and we earn a small cut. Finding your own money is always free."
If it's free, isn't the product the user / their data?
"No — that's exactly what we refuse to be. We make money the honest way: a cut when you choose to buy through us, and offers you can ignore. Your data is never the product."
What does it cost you to run?
"Under 10 cents to scan an entire inbox. Pure software — no inventory, no shipping — so it scales to millions without our costs really moving."
How big can this get?
"The US gift card market is $234 billion a year, and $23 billion sits unused right now. We don't need a huge slice — even a small cut of money currently going to waste is a real business."
Why would brands pay you for featured offers?
"Because we send them customers about to spend. Someone who just found $50 they forgot they had is in buying mode — that's the most valuable moment to put an offer in front of."

Tier 3 — Growth & customers

How do you get your first customers?
"We start at our own school — get it dense, everyone finding money, then go school by school. One network at a time, exactly how Facebook started."
Paid ads or social?
"Organic only — no ad budget. The find-money moment is the perfect TikTok. Someone screenshots 'I just found $300' and that's the ad. The product makes its own content."
Isn't word of mouth slow?
"Not when the payoff is instant cash. People don't sit on 'I just found $300' — they share it the second it happens. Fastest growth there is."
What if people try it once and never come back?
"New gift cards keep landing — birthdays, rewards, refunds — and Stash keeps catching them and reminding you before they expire. A reason to come back every month."

Tier 4 — Product / tech / accuracy

How does it actually work?
"You connect your email read-only. Stash searches for gift-card emails, an AI reads each one to pull the store, balance, and code, and shows them all in one wallet — one tap to use each. You can also snap a photo of a plastic card."
What if it misses cards or gets the amount wrong?
"We've tested it on real inboxes — it found $434 across six cards on mine with zero false matches. Not perfect yet, but it already finds money people completely forgot, and it gets better with every inbox."
Can you see the current balance, or just the original amount?
"We always show the original value, and for a growing list of stores we fetch the live balance automatically. There's no universal balance API — so we're honest: original up front, live balance where we can get it."
What about physical plastic cards?
"Snap a photo, front and back. It reads the number, barcode, even the PIN, and adds it to your wallet. Plastic and digital in one place."
Did you build this or is it a mockup?
"It's real and live — stashloot.vercel.app. What you saw scan was the actual product on a real inbox, not a slideshow."

Tier 5 — Market / demand proof

Your survey was small — is the demand real?
"Small sample, but unanimous — every group said the same thing, and it lines up with the national number: $21 billion a year unused. Our responses just confirmed what the whole country already shows."
Do teens really have enough in gift cards to matter?
"To a teen, $80 found is huge. But teens are our wedge — most digital, most scattered. The real market is everyone: Gen X averages $255 unused. $80 to the user, $21 billion as a business."
Who cares about a $20 card?
"Nobody cares about one $20 card — that's exactly why it's a $21 billion problem. Everyone ignores small amounts and companies count on it. We make ignoring it impossible."

Tier 6 — Team / why you

Why are you three the right people to build this?
"We built it because it happened to us — tired of finding expired cards with money still on them. We shipped a working product in days, not months. We move fast and we use the thing we're building."
What happens after camp — is this real or a project?
"It's live right now and we're not stopping. Plan is to seed our own school this fall and grow from there."

Tier 7 — Adversarial curveballs

This sounds illegal / like you're stealing from companies.
"Nothing illegal — the money is legally yours, it's your gift card. We're not taking from companies; we're stopping them from banking on you forgetting. That bet has a name in their own filings: breakage. We just end the bet."
Gift cards legally can't expire for 5 years — so what's the problem?
"Exactly — the money doesn't disappear, it gets forgotten. The law protects the balance; it doesn't make you remember the card. That gap — forgotten money that's still valid — is the whole problem we solve."
What's your biggest weakness / what could kill this?
"Trust — convincing people to connect their email. That's why read-only and 'we never sell your data' aren't fine print, they're the front of the brand. We win by being the one money app people actually trust."
You can't pull cash out — so what does "take it back" mean?
"Right — a gift card is store credit, you can't cash it out, and we don't pretend you can. 'Take it back' means using it before the company keeps it. We find it, hand you the code, one tap to spend."
What if Google shuts off your email access?
"We use the official read-only Gmail scope the way Google intends, so we're inside their rules. And we're not locked to Gmail — the same model works on Outlook, plus a forward-your-email path that needs no login."
(If the demo fails live) Your app just broke.
"Live demos and camp wifi — classic. Here's the clean scan we recorded earlier (roll backup video). Same product, same $434.50." Stay calm, smile, never apologize twice.

Closer
