# STASH — Q&A Battle Card (INC Pitch, Sat 2026-06-13)

> Rehearse these out loud until they're reflexes. Rule: **answer in one breath, then stop.** Don't over-explain — a short confident answer beats a long defensive one. If you don't know, say "great question — here's how we'd find out," never bluff.

> Golden rules under fire:
> - **Stay Robin Hood.** Never say "targeted ads," "sell data," "CAC," "go-to-market." Say "featured offers," "how it spreads," "first users."
> - **Don't say "steal/illegal."** Say companies are "banking on you forgetting." Breakage is in their public filings — unattackable.
> - **The card is the proof, not the pitch.** When cornered, point back to the live $434.50.

## 🔒 TIER 1 — MOST LIKELY + MOST DANGEROUS (drill these hardest)

### Privacy / "I'm not giving you my email"
**Q: Why would anyone give you access to their email? That's scary.**
> "Totally fair — that's the first thing people ask. Stash is **read-only**. We can't send, delete, or change anything. We only ever look for gift cards — nothing else is touched. You sign in through Google, so we never see your password, and you can disconnect in one tap. It's the same Google sign-in you already use for a hundred apps."

**Q: How do I know you're not reading my personal emails / selling my data?**
> "We never sell your data — that's a promise the whole brand is built on. Technically we only request the read-only gift-card scope from Google, and Google enforces it — we *couldn't* read your personal mail even if we wanted to. Our money comes from featured offers and our in-app shop, not from your inbox."

**Q: What happens to the data after you scan?**
> "Your cards live on your device, not our server. The scan reads, extracts your gift cards, and shows them to you — we're not building a profile. Encrypted in transit, stored on your phone, revocable anytime."
> *(Never claim "end-to-end encrypted" — false; we read text server-side to extract. Say "encrypted in transit + stored on your device.")*

### Competition / moat
**Q: What's stopping Google/Apple/Amazon from just building this?**
> "They could build the feature — but it'd be a footnote in a giant app. For us it's the *whole* company, and we start where they won't bother: students, one school at a time. By the time it's worth their attention, we own the network and the brand. Same way Venmo beat the banks at payments before the banks woke up."

**Q: How are you different from [Gyft / Prezzee / CardCash]?**
> "Every one of them starts with a card *you already know you have*. Gyft makes you upload each one by hand. Prezzee just sells you new ones. CardCash buys yours for less than they're worth. **Stash is the only one that finds the money you forgot about — automatically.** That's the whole difference."

**Q: Isn't this easy to copy?**
> "The feature, maybe. The network, no. Our moat is being first and dense in a school — once your whole friend group is on Stash, a copycat starts at zero. And the more inboxes we scan, the smarter our finder gets. First-mover + a product that improves with every user."

### "Isn't it just a..."
**Q: Isn't this just a reminder app?**
> "You can't remind yourself about money you forgot you had. We *find* it first — that's the part you can't do yourself — then we delete every excuse not to use it. Cal AI didn't invent calorie counting; it deleted the friction. Same move."

**Q: Isn't this just a wallet / Apple Wallet already does this?**
> "Apple Wallet only holds what you manually add. It has no idea about the $50 Nike card buried in your inbox from two birthdays ago. We find what you'd never think to add."

## 💰 TIER 2 — BUSINESS MODEL & MONEY

**Q: How do you actually make money?**
> "Two ways. Today — featured offers from brands you'd actually use. Next — the Stash Shop, where you buy and send gift cards right in the app and we earn a small cut. Finding your own money is always free."

**Q: If it's free, isn't the product the user / their data?**
> "No — that's exactly what we refuse to be. We make money the honest way: a cut when you *choose* to buy a gift card through us, and offers you can ignore. Your data is never the product."

**Q: What does it cost you to run?**
> "Under 10 cents to scan an entire inbox. It's pure software — no inventory, no shipping — so it scales to millions without our costs really moving."

**Q: How big can this actually get / what's the business worth?**
> "The US gift card market is $234 billion a year, and $23 billion sits unused right now. We don't need a huge slice — even a small cut of money that's currently going to waste is a real business."

**Q: Why would brands pay you for featured offers?**
> "Because we send them customers who are about to spend — someone who just found $50 they forgot they had is in buying mode. That's the most valuable moment to put an offer in front of."

## 📈 TIER 3 — GROWTH & CUSTOMERS

**Q: How do you get your first customers?**
> "We start at our own school — get it dense, everyone finding money, then go school by school. One network at a time, exactly how Facebook started."

**Q: Paid ads or social?**
> "Organic only — no ad budget. The find-money moment *is* the perfect TikTok. Someone screenshots 'I just found $300' and that's the ad. The product makes its own content."

**Q: Isn't word of mouth slow?**
> "Not when the payoff is instant cash. People don't sit on 'I just found $300' — they share it the second it happens. Fastest growth there is."

**Q: What if people try it once and never come back?**
> "That's why it's not just a one-time scan. New gift cards keep landing in your inbox — birthdays, rewards, refunds — and Stash keeps catching them and reminding you before they expire. It's a reason to come back every month."

## 🧠 TIER 4 — PRODUCT / TECH / ACCURACY

**Q: How does it actually work?**
> "You connect your email read-only. Stash searches for gift-card emails, an AI reads each one to pull the store, balance, and code, and it shows them all in one wallet — with one tap to use each. You can also snap a photo of a plastic card and it reads that too."

**Q: What if it misses cards or gets the amount wrong?**
> "We've tested it on real inboxes — it found $434 across six cards on mine with zero false matches. It's not perfect yet, but it already finds money people had completely forgotten. And it gets better with every inbox we scan."

**Q: Can you see the *current* balance, or just the original amount?**
> "We always show what the card was worth, and for a growing list of stores we can fetch the live balance automatically. There's no universal balance API across all brands — so we're honest: original value up front, live balance where we can get it."

**Q: What about physical plastic cards?**
> "Snap a photo — front and back. It reads the number, the barcode, even the PIN off the back, and adds it to your wallet. Now your plastic and digital cards live in one place."

**Q: Did you build this or is it a mockup?**
> "It's real and live — stashloot.vercel.app. What you just saw scan was the actual product on a real inbox, not a slideshow."

## 📊 TIER 5 — MARKET / DEMAND PROOF

**Q: Your survey was small — is the demand real?**
> "Small sample, but it was unanimous — every group said the same thing, and it lines up exactly with the national numbers: $21 billion a year goes unused. Our 20 responses just confirmed what the whole country already shows."

**Q: Do teens really have enough in gift cards to matter?**
> "To a teen, $80 found is huge. But teens are just our wedge — the most digital, most scattered users. The real market is everyone: Gen X averages $255 unused. $80 to the user, $21 billion as a business."

**Q: Isn't this a tiny problem / who cares about a $20 card?**
> "Nobody cares about one $20 card — that's exactly why it's a $21 billion problem. Everyone ignores small amounts, and companies count on it. We make ignoring it impossible."

## ⚠️ TIER 7 — ADVERSARIAL CURVEBALLS (the ones that try to rattle you)

**Q: This sounds illegal / like you're stealing from companies.**
> "Nothing illegal — the money is legally yours, it's your gift card. We're not taking from companies; we're stopping them from *banking on you forgetting*. That bet has a name in their own filings: breakage. We just end the bet."

**Q: Gift cards legally can't expire for 5 years — so what's the problem?**
> "Exactly — the money doesn't disappear, it just gets *forgotten*. The card law protects the balance; it doesn't make you remember the card. That gap — forgotten money that's still valid — is the entire problem we solve."

**Q: What's your biggest weakness / what could kill this?**
> "Trust — convincing people to connect their email. That's why read-only and 'we never sell your data' aren't fine print, they're the front of the brand. We win by being the one money app people actually trust."

**Q: You can't pull cash out — so what does 'take it back' even mean?**
> "Right — a gift card is store credit, you can't cash it out, and we don't pretend you can. 'Take it back' means *using it* before the company keeps it. We find it, hand you the code, one tap to spend. Redeeming it is how you beat the breakage bet."

**Q: What if Google shuts off your email access?**
> "We only use the official read-only Gmail scope the way Google intends, so we're inside their rules. And we're not locked to Gmail — the same model works on Outlook, and we have a forward-your-email path that needs no login at all."

**Q: (If demo fails live) — your app just broke.**
> "Live demos and camp wifi — classic. Here's the clean scan we recorded earlier *(roll 20-30s backup video)*. Same product, same $434.50." *(Stay calm, smile, never apologize twice.)*
